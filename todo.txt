since this is a teaching game, does it make sense to have repeated assignments of the same tasks over 10 days

No, I don't think it does. It's a good catch, and it changes one of my earlier recommendations.

Why the repetition doesn't teach much: From the code, assignments clear at the end of every workday. A two-day task like the timber frame has to be assigned again on day two, and the same goes for the canopy roof. The game is trying to teach three things: matching skills to tasks, managing stamina, and sequencing dependent work. A player gets all three right the first time they assign a task, and the second tap teaches nothing new.

The repetition may also teach the wrong lesson. In real projects, you assign a task once and then check in and step in when something changes, which is called management by exception. Daily re-tapping suggests the job is micromanaging.

When daily decisions are worth it: Repeating a step is fine when the decision actually changes, and a few things here do make it change:

Stamina: a rested artisan is a different choice from a worn-out one.
Events and blocked tasks: a delay or a newly unblocked task shifts priorities.
Rest timing: this is the most interesting daily decision in the game.

What I'd do: Make assignments persist. A task stays with its artisan until it's finished or the player changes it, and the player only re-engages when the situation changes (stamina low, a task unblocked, an event). A "run until something needs attention" option would be a natural next step, and it matches management by exception.

A correction to my earlier list: I suggested an "Assign suggested crew" button. I'd drop it. Suggestions would automate skill matching, which is one of the few decisions that actually teaches something. Persistence removes the boring part and keeps the real decisions in the player's hands.
